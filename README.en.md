# NeoBestiary Studio

Describe a creature in French (or English), and NeoBestiary Studio draws it as a **64×64 game character**, seen from above, in **4 directions** with a **5-frame walk**, ready for a game, and writes its **bestiary entry**.

*Version française : [README.md](README.md)*

![NeoBestiary Studio](screenshots/1.jpg)

## What it does

- **Inspiration**: out of ideas? Pick a theme (Lovecraft, Animals, Monsters, World folklore, Yokai from Japanese folklore, Video game archetypes, Anime archetypes, Living objects such as mimics and tsukumogami, or Mix: over 200 inspirations, without copying an existing creature) and click "Inspire me": you get a name and a clear description written for drawing (shape, posture, number of eyes or legs, colours). Edit it if you like.
- **Endless summoning**: the Studio picks a random theme by itself, invents the creature, keeps the best sketch and finishes it completely, then moves on to the next one. Press the button again to stop: the current creature is finished first ("Stop" cuts right away).
- **Create**: write what you want ("just a giant eye", "a cow with a single horn", "a bull with a spider's body"…), with nothing imposed or forbidden. The app first draws **3 sketches** in half a minute: you pick the most faithful one, and the back, the side view and the walk are made from it. Before drawing, the app reads your description and works out the posture (on four legs, standing, many-legged, flying, floating, crawling, swimming, hopping, rolling into a ball, sliding like a jelly, rooted) and the number of legs; a posture written out ("crawling") is always respected. It tells you what it understood, and the back and side views follow it: each posture has its own side-view rule (a snake stays flat on the ground, a quadruped horizontal, a floating creature stays in the air, with no invented legs). Seen from behind, left and right are swapped, as when you look at the creature from the back. It also details each part with its number and place ("three heads, each on its own neck") and knows the traits of classic monsters and objects: hydra, cerberus, mimic, beholder-like eye tyrant, chimera, kitsune, golem, lich, haunted armour… Your numbers and colours always win.
- **Automatic check**: each sketch is looked at by a vision model, which counts the heads and checks that a legless creature has no legs. A failed sketch ("1 head instead of 3") is redone by itself, twice at most; under each sketch you see "✓ checked" or what is wrong. The side view of a floating, swimming or crawling creature is checked the same way: a side view with legs is discarded and redone.
- **Targeted touch-up**: on a sketch, the front, the back or the side view, "Touch up" opens the image. Click the area to change (small, medium, large, or the whole creature) and write "add a horn here" or "make the eyes red": only that part is redrawn. The touch-up becomes a new version, the old one stays with ◀ ▶, and the walk, the mirror and the attacks follow.
  - **Orientation is fixed in plain words**: "it's upside down", "it's not the back but the front", "we see its back", "it's looking right", "head down". The app understands what you mean: a side view facing the wrong way is mirrored (exactly, without redrawing), an upside-down creature is turned the right way up, and a front that shows the back (or a back that shows the face) is redrawn in the right orientation, with a stronger instruction and two attempts. The rest of your sentence ("… and the eyes red") is then applied as a normal touch-up.
- **Cut-out**: the automatic background removal is finished by the app (clean edge, no grey fringe, no stray crumbs). If a flaw remains, "Cut-out" opens the image: the eraser removes what sticks out, "Restore" brings back what was eaten. The result becomes a new version.
- **64×64 images**: the downscaling keeps small high-contrast details, so eyes and pupils no longer disappear into the skin.
- **5-frame walk per direction**: step, half-step, rest, half-step, step (played in a loop over 8 beats), for smooth, readable motion. The legs of the resting pose are animated (one side then the other from the front and back, front and back legs swinging in the side view), and the body dips by one point on each footfall. Creatures made earlier get their half-steps by themselves. The creature stays identical from frame to frame, same points, same colours. Others move according to their posture: a winged creature that flies or floats flaps its wings, a snake or worm undulates, a fish swishes its tail, a frog crouches and leaps, a ball rolls, a jelly spreads and contracts, a plant sways. This motion continues while standing still.
- **Type**: the writer suggests two or three types, the description decides (stone, feathers, venom, lightning…), and on a tie the type your bestiary lacks wins: ice, thunder, steel or light end up appearing instead of always shadow or spirit. **One creature in two has two types** ("fire and wind"): the second is chosen the same way among the others. At least one of its attacks has the second type. Shadow and light together: NeoMe only. A hybrid takes the first type of each parent. Creatures made earlier received their second type, one in two, without rewriting their entry.
- **Bestiary entry**: at the end of creation, an encyclopedia-style entry is written by itself: type, species, size, weight, habitat, description and a short story. It is shown with the **bestiary image**: the creature at rest, whole, in its natural posture. Three images are drawn and the vision model keeps the one most faithful to the description (with its score and what is missing); "Redo the image" offers others. "Rewrite the entry" offers another one.
- **Combat**: the entry gives a **rarity** (common, uncommon, rare, epic, legendary), **stats** (HP, attack, defence, speed), **weaknesses**, **resistances** and **immunities** drawn from a **type chart**: each attack element is ×2, ×½ or has no effect against each type (lightning does nothing to rock), each cell with its reason (water puts out fire and rusts metal, roots split rock, frost cracks stone, wind blows away miasma, light drives off ghosts…), and for a dual type both multiply: ×4, ×¼, or a weakness the other type resists that comes back to ×1. Rarity depends on the creature's **prestige** (how impressive it is: size, heads, powers, legendary side): an ancient dragon is never common. Once the bestiary holds at least 10 creatures, it comes from ranking creatures against each other, to keep about one creature in two common and one in fifty legendary. You can also set it by hand in the entry. The rarer it is, the higher its stats.
- **Attacks**: **2 to 4 attacks** depending on rarity, all different, chosen from the body (tail: whip, horns: horn strike, tentacles: grip, wings: gust…). 17 kinds: claw, bite, charge, whip, horn, sting, grip, projectile, breath, beam, cry, gust, rain, wave, explosion, heal and boost. Each move crossed with each of the 12 elements gives a separate attack with its own name and mechanics: **204 attacks** in all. They can inflict a status with a percentage chance (burn, poison, freeze, paralysis, sleep, confusion, bleeding, dazzle, fear), lower the enemy's attack, defence, speed or accuracy, raise your own, hit several times, move first, drain HP, cost recoil, aim for critical hits or cure statuses. The entry spells it out ("25% chance: freeze · accuracy 90%"). Entries written earlier get their mechanics at startup, without changing their moves. Each attack is animated in 4 directions, in 4 frames, with its own move and visual effect. **120 signature visuals**: for claws, bites, charges, whips, projectiles, breaths, beams, rains, waves and explosions, each element has its own effect drawn with its own motif (embers, drops, leaves, swirls, rock shards, crystals, lightning, sparks, shadows, orbs, blades, bubbles), so the Ember rain, the Hail and the Star rain no longer look alike. Other moves keep an effect in the element's colours. In the in-game preview, keys 1 to 4 launch them.
- **Attack rarities**: each catalogue attack has a rarity based on what it is worth (expected damage, status, stat stages, priority, healing): about half are common, 4 legendary. NeoMe's signature attacks are **???**: no other creature can have them. A creature can have attacks up to **2 steps above its own rarity** (a common one: up to rare), both when drawn and in the menu. In the library, every catalogue attack replays with its staging (caster, path, impact).
- **Types and attacks by hand**: in the entry, choose one or two types (weaknesses and attack elements follow, rarity does not move) and replace any attack with one of the 204 in the catalogue or with **one of your own created attacks** (Create › Attack): it keeps its name, sheet and staging, in Directions as in the Arena, with the same rarity limit. These choices hold when the entry is recalculated or rewritten.
- **Type chosen before the creature**: in Create, choose a type or leave **Auto**: endless summoning and "Inspire me" first draw a type, more often the ones the bestiary lacks (ice, thunder, steel…), then invent and draw the creature around it.
- **Entry image**: the image at the top is the model's front render of the creature (the export also includes it: `face.png` and `face_hd.png`).
- **NeoMe, creature #0**: NeoDex's companion, exactly as it is, in 4 directions. Type **shadow and light**, rarity **???** (above legendary, fixed), the best stats in the bestiary and four attacks that belong to it alone, with their own effects.
- **Combat animations**: besides walking and attacks, each creature breathes and blinks while waiting, takes hits (white flash, red-tinted recoil) and gets knocked out (tips over, collapses, disappears). Everything is made from its own image, in 4 directions.
- **Combat portrait**: a large 256×256 illustration of the creature mid-attack, for the encounter screen and the entry. The number of heads is checked (a portrait hiding one is redone); "Redo the portrait" offers another, ◀ ▶ keeps the old ones. It is shown in the entry next to the stats.
- **Colour variants**: the same creature repainted in an instant, without a graphics card: **chromatic** (hues rotated, one rarity step higher) or an **element** (ice, fire, shadow, poison…), whose type, weaknesses and attack colour it then takes. Each variant has its own images, attacks and animations.
- **Fusion**: in the Library, "Fuse" then two finished creatures. The writer invents the hybrid (name and description), the sketches start from both designs, and the entry inherits the type of one parent, the quick attacks of one and the strongest of the other, and the prestige of the more impressive one.
- **Balance**: a tab showing the spread of rarities (and the expected share), types, attack rarities and moves, and the average stats of your whole bestiary, with a ranking and what is off: "no ice creature", "too many legendaries"… It also suggests rarities from each creature's prestige ("Hydra: common → epic"), applied in one click.
- **World**: a world generator for your game, no graphics card needed. A seed always gives the same world: continents or archipelago, terrain, climate (from jungle to eternal snow), rivers running down to the sea, cities by the water, and 3 to 8 factions with their capital and borders. The pixel-art map zooms with the wheel and moves with the mouse; a click shows the place (biome, altitude, faction, nearest city) and **the creatures of your library that live there**, from their type (fire at the volcano and in the desert, ice in the snow…). Keep your worlds in a list, export the map as PNG or the whole world as JSON (lands, factions, cities, biomes and wildlife) for your game.
- **NeoBestiary Arena**: each finished creature has an `arene.json` file (combat entry, sheets, animations, variants), read by the NeoBestiary Arena app to make it fight.
- **Directions**: the sheet of 4 directions × 3 steps. Each frame has its versions (◀ ▶) and each direction can be redone; redoing the front redoes the whole creature.
- **`Sprite Lab`** (the touch-up tab):
  - a point-by-point 64×64 editor (pencil, eraser, eyedropper, bucket, undo / redo);
  - the palette shared by the 20 frames, the outline;
  - an in-game preview: your creature walks on grass with the arrow keys.
- **Export**: each exported creature goes into the `Exports` folder (button in the Library), one subfolder per creature: the PNG sheet (3 columns × 4 rows of 64×64), its JSON (directions, frames, anchor point at the feet, animation order, entry), the bestiary image (256×256 and large), the combat portrait, for each attack the body sheet, the effect sheet and their offset in the JSON, the idle / hit / K.O. sheets, and each variant in `variantes/`.
- **Four kinds of creation**: in Create, choose **Creature**, **Object**, **Attack** or **Music**. Only one creation runs at a time (the graphics card handles one): while one is being drawn, music waits, and the Studio says which is running.
  - **Object**: describe it ("a gold pocket watch with a cracked face"), choose its category (weapon, potion, jewel, everyday object…) and its rarity. You get a 64×64 icon in **3 versions** to choose from (◀ ▶).
  - **Attack**: describe its effect, choose the element and the move. The model draws the energy and the impact, animated as a sheet of 10 frames × 3 phases (cast, travel, impact), like NeoMe's attacks; the mechanics come from the catalogue (damage, status, chances). The preview replays it with its staging.
  - **Inspire me** works here too: a name and a description matching your choices (category and rarity, element and move, tempo and mood).
  - **Music**: describe the style, add lyrics or leave empty for an instrumental, choose the length (30 s to 3 min), the tempo and the mood. It comes out as an MP3, with a player in the app.
- **Library**: creatures, objects, attacks and music, with search, **favourites** (the star on each card; favourites come first, the "Favourites" button shows only them) and **sorting** by date, name, rarity or type. Attacks include the **204 in the catalogue** and NeoMe's signature attacks. A deleted creation goes to a bin, it is not erased.

Creatures are stored in `Documents\NeoBestiary Studio`, objects, attacks and music in its `objets`, `attaques` and `musiques` folders, exports in `Documents\NeoBestiary Studio\Exports`.

## Requirements

- Windows and an **NVIDIA graphics card with 8 GB of video memory** or more.
- **ComfyUI portable** ([github.com/comfyanonymous/ComfyUI](https://github.com/comfyanonymous/ComfyUI), Releases page), unzipped anywhere. On first launch, the app looks for it and asks for its folder if needed.
- The models, all local, which the app **installs by itself** as soon as it opens (open licences, from Hugging Face, about 25 GB in all; music downloads in the background without blocking creations):
  - **FLUX.2 klein 4B** (drawing);
  - the **Qwen3 4B** encoder (it understands French);
  - **BiRefNet** (cut-out: no background, no ground shadow);
  - **ACE-Step 1.5 turbo** (music, about 9 GB).
- For inspiration and entries: **Ollama** ([ollama.com](https://ollama.com)) with a text model, for example `ollama pull mistral-nemo` (about 7 GB, very good in French). For the automatic check, a model that sees images: `ollama pull gemma3:4b` (about 3 GB). Without Ollama everything else works; only inspiration, entries and the check wait, and the posture is guessed from keywords.

ComfyUI starts by itself on the first summoning and stops when you close NeoDex. If you already started your own ComfyUI, the app uses it and does not stop it.

## How long it takes

On an RTX 4060: half a minute for the 3 sketches, then about 2 minutes for the back, the side view (two attempts each, the best kept automatically), the walk and the entry. The check adds about ten seconds, plus the time of a redone sketch when one is needed; the combat portrait about twenty seconds. An inspiration idea takes about ten seconds (a minute the first time, while the text model loads). An object (3 versions) or an attack: under a minute.

## Permissions

The app asks for **full access**: it runs the drawing engine (Python, shipped with the plugin), ComfyUI and talks to Ollama on your PC, and saves your creatures in your Documents. Nothing goes to the Internet except the model downloads.

## Data and sync

Nothing is synced: creatures, models and images stay on the PC that has the graphics card.

## Install

In NeoDex: **NeoStore → Discover → NeoBestiary Studio → Install**.
